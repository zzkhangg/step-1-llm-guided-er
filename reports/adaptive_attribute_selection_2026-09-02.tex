\section{The Matcher Drifts, and the Drift Is Now Measured Directly}
\label{sec:drift}

Week 5 inferred provider drift from the fact that F1 levels moved while the pipeline did not.
It can be measured without that inference. The August and late-August response caches share
\textbf{69{,}087 Amazon--Walmart prompts that are identical byte for byte}. Replaying the
same prompt to the same model slug on the two dates:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Identical prompts compared        & 69{,}087 \\
Answered Yes in August            & 1{,}589 \\
Answered Yes in late August       & 3{,}250 \\
Moved No $\rightarrow$ Yes        & 2{,}269 \\
Moved Yes $\rightarrow$ No        & 608 \\
Disagreement rate                 & 4.16\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The same input, the same model slug, two dates. The imbalance is 3.7 to 1 toward
Yes, so this is a directional shift and not sampling noise.}
\end{table}

The consequence is visible in one configuration held constant across three dates
(Step 1.5, decisive, \texttt{gap}, $k \ge 1$, all 88{,}296 Amazon--Walmart pairs):

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Date & Precision & Recall & F1 \\
\midrule
19 August & 0.4762 & 0.7962 & 0.5959 \\
20 August & 0.4986 & 0.7997 & 0.6142 \\
31 August & 0.2215 & 0.8093 & \textbf{0.3478} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Identical code, identical prompts, identical model slug. Precision more than halves;
recall does not move, because a matcher answering Yes more often loses precision and keeps
recall.}
\end{table}

\textbf{The routing behind this is now observed rather than inferred.} Until this week
nothing in the recorded response could name the cause, so provider variation was a
hypothesis. OpenRouter returns a non-standard \texttt{provider} field on each completion, and
the pipeline now records it per call, into the response cache and into
\texttt{final\_results.csv}, with a per-run histogram in the run summary. Forty calls issued
back to back against the single slug \texttt{deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash-0731} were served by
\textbf{eleven distinct providers}: OpenInference (22\%), DigitalOcean (18\%), Ambient and
Relace (12\% each), DeepInfra (10\%), Inceptron (8\%), Together and StreamLake (5\% each),
and Reka, Fireworks and Sail Research (one call each). One slug is therefore not one model in
any operational sense, and no run of this pipeline before this week can say which mixture
produced it.

\textbf{No comparison may place its two arms on different dates.} Every run reported below
was executed inside a single window for this reason.

\textbf{Amazon--Walmart is the noisier benchmark, not the calmer one.} Its seven times larger
pair count might be expected to average random flips away. Two runs of no selection over an
identical fixed slice of 20{,}000 pairs, both on 27 August, disagree on 1{,}040 of them ---
\textbf{5.20\%}, above DBLP--ACM's 4.82\% --- and on \textbf{0.078 F1}, 0.3299 against
0.4083. Several differences reported for this dataset in Week 5 are smaller than that.

\section{Repeating the Amazon--Walmart Comparison Dissolves It}
\label{sec:amazon}

The Week 5 table separates no selection from adaptive selection by 0.023 F1, against a
run-to-run spread of 0.078. The comparison was repeated three times per arm on a fixed random
slice of 20{,}000 candidate pairs (seed 7, containing 233 of the 1{,}143 ground-truth
matches), all six runs inside one day, each writing its own response cache so that every draw
is independent rather than a replay.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrl}
\toprule
Configuration & Draw 1 & Draw 2 & Draw 3 & Mean & Range \\
\midrule
No selection (\texttt{full})       & 0.3442 & 0.4311 & 0.3953 & 0.3902 & [0.3442, 0.4311] \\
Step 1.5, decisive (\texttt{gap})  & 0.3204 & 0.3820 & 0.3945 & 0.3656 & [0.3204, 0.3945] \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Amazon--Walmart F1 over three independent draws per arm, 31 August--1 September.
Levels are not comparable with the Week 5 table, which scores over all 88{,}296 pairs and was
run three weeks earlier.}
\end{table}

\textbf{The ranges overlap almost completely, and the paired tests do not agree on a sign.}
Pairing the draws in order gives no selection uniquely correct on 755 pairs against 725
($p = 0.45$), then 588 against 514 ($p = 0.028$), then 538 against 594 ($p = 0.10$) --- a
tie, a win for no selection, and a lead for adaptive selection, from three repetitions of one
experiment. The two configurations are not distinguishable on this dataset, and the 0.023 F1
advantage reported in Week 5 is a property of which draw was taken. \textbf{That ordering is
withdrawn.}

\textbf{Two quantities do survive repetition.}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
 & No selection & Step 1.5, decisive \\
\midrule
Recall (range over 3 draws)    & [0.9313, 0.9528] & [0.8670, 0.8755] \\
Precision (range over 3 draws) & [0.2111, 0.2785] & [0.1965, 0.2554] \\
Pipeline tokens (mean)         & 20{,}607{,}108   & \textbf{5{,}186{,}455} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Recall separates cleanly and in the same direction every draw --- a consistent cost
of roughly 0.074 --- while precision overlaps, so nothing is bought back. Cost separates by
far more: a 74.8\% reduction, of which profiling consumes 55{,}448 tokens.}
\end{table}

The defensible claim on this schema is therefore narrower than Week 5 states it.
Pair-adaptive selection removes three quarters of the token cost with no measurable change in
F1 and a reliable loss of recall. It is a cost--quality trade, not an accuracy result, and it
stands in deliberate contrast to DBLP--ACM, where the same configuration separates from no
selection by 0.186 with non-overlapping ranges.

\section{Blank Fields: Removing Them and Hiding Them Are Two Different Things}
\label{sec:mask}

The evidence mask of Week 5 zeroes an attribute's importance when either record leaves it
blank, which bars it from being \emph{selected}. As implemented it also keeps the field out of
the prompt entirely. Those are separable operations, and the distinction was raised by the
supervisor: a matcher shown \texttt{modelno} as an empty field can read it as unknown,
whereas one never shown the field cannot distinguish a missing value from a schema without
that column.

A second mode, \texttt{keep}, bars the attribute from selection exactly as before but appends
it, blank, to the prompt. Selection is byte-identical under both, so the runs differ only in
what the matcher reads. Both were run back to back on one day over all 88{,}296 pairs, sharing
a response cache so that the 62{,}770 pairs whose prompt is unchanged replay rather than being
re-drawn.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
Blank fields in the prompt & Precision & Recall & F1 & TP & FP \\
\midrule
Removed (\texttt{drop})                & 0.2215 & 0.8093 & 0.3478 & 925 & 3{,}251 \\
Shown (\texttt{keep})                  & 0.2017 & 0.8119 & 0.3231 & 928 & 3{,}674 \\
Shown only when blank on both sides    & \textbf{0.2251} & 0.8075 & \textbf{0.3521} & 923 & \textbf{3{,}177} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Amazon--Walmart, all three runs 31 August--1 September. Levels sit far below the
same configuration's 0.6142 in Week 5 for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:drift}; only
the within-table comparison is meaningful.}
\end{table}

Showing every blank field costs 423 false positives to buy 3 true ones, and loses the paired
test 1{,}155 uniquely correct pairs to 737 ($p = 6.2\times10^{-22}$) over the 25{,}526 pairs
whose prompt differs. \textbf{Splitting those pairs by which kind of blank was restored shows
that the single answer was hiding two opposite ones.}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
Restored attribute was & Pairs & Gold matches & \texttt{drop} right & \texttt{keep} right & $p$ \\
\midrule
blank on one side  & 12{,}894 & 242 (1.88\%) & \textbf{999} & 504 & $7.5\times10^{-38}$ \\
blank on both      & 12{,}560 &  14 (0.11\%) & 157 & \textbf{233} & $1.1\times10^{-4}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The two kinds of missingness carry opposite signal.}
\end{table}

The gold labels explain the reversal without reference to the matcher. Against a base match
rate of 1.18\% over the candidate pairs, a field blank on exactly one side is mildly
\emph{pro}-match, while a field blank on both is strongly anti-match:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Attribute & Both sides present & \textbf{One side blank} & Both sides blank \\
\midrule
\texttt{modelno}    & 1.26\% & \textbf{1.88\%} & 0.11\% \\
\texttt{shortdescr} & 1.10\% & \textbf{1.97\%} & 0.35\% \\
\texttt{dimensions} & 1.27\% & \textbf{2.05\%} & 0.52\% \\
\texttt{longdescr}  & 1.17\% & \textbf{2.74\%} & 0.00\% \\
\texttt{brand}      & 1.18\% & \textbf{1.85\%} & 0.00\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Match rate among the 88{,}296 Amazon--Walmart candidate pairs, base rate 1.18\%. A
sparsely catalogued record is more often a genuine second listing of the same product than a
different product described in full, which is why a one-sided blank is not evidence against a
match. A field neither record carries is: \texttt{modelno} at 0.11\% is nearly ten times below
base. No LLM calls were needed to produce this table.}
\end{table}

Showing a half-filled field invites agreement it has not earned; showing that neither record
has the field is a genuine reason to doubt. A third mode keeping only the second case wins the
paired test against \texttt{drop}, 233 to 157 over 12{,}632 affected pairs
($p = 1.4\times10^{-4}$), and is implemented as \texttt{-{-}evidence-mask-mode both\_blank}.

\textbf{This is reported as mechanism, not as an improvement, and \texttt{drop} remains the
default everywhere else.} The margin is 0.0043 F1 against the 0.078 spread two identical runs
exhibit on this dataset; it was found by inspecting the evaluation set's labels with no
held-out split; and on DBLP--ACM the mask binds on 5 of 13{,}080 pairs, so the distinction
cannot matter there. What the split contributes is the finding that missingness is not one
signal but two, pointing in opposite directions --- which the current feature vector
represents identically.

\section{What Changes in the Thesis Narrative}

\textbf{The Amazon--Walmart claim is reduced from accuracy to cost.} The dataset now supports
one statement: pair-adaptive selection removes 74.8\% of the token cost with no measurable
change in F1 and a repeatable 0.074 loss of recall. The 0.023 advantage claimed for no
selection, and the implication that adaptive selection trails there, are both withdrawn.

\textbf{Having two datasets reach different conclusions is the stronger result.} DBLP--ACM
separates by 0.186 with disjoint ranges; Amazon--Walmart does not separate at all. Together
they bound where the method applies --- aggressive condensation wins outright where one or
two attributes can carry the decision, and degrades to a cost saving where the evidence is
spread across a wide, sparsely populated schema. A report claiming a win on both would be
harder to defend, not easier.

\textbf{Non-reproducibility of a commercial LLM matcher is a finding in its own right, and
its mechanism is now identified.} Two identical runs disagree on 5.2\% of pairs and 0.078 F1;
69{,}087 identical prompts replayed a fortnight later move from 1{,}589 Yes answers to
3{,}250; and forty consecutive calls to one model slug are served by eleven different
providers. Any result this pipeline obtains through a hosted model API inherits this, and the
provider is not recoverable after the fact from a run that did not record it --- as none of
this project's runs before this week did. Whether related work reports this is not something
this report has checked, and no claim is made about it. Condensation halves the variance
(Week 5), which is a second argument for the method that has nothing to do with accuracy.

\section{Limitations Added or Changed This Week}

\textbf{One headline comparison did not survive repetition.} Both headline rows are now
repeated three times on each dataset. On DBLP--ACM the ordering is stable and the ranges
disjoint; on Amazon--Walmart the ranges overlap and the sign of the paired test changes
between draws. Every remaining row --- the whole of Fodors--Zagat, the six other
Amazon--Walmart configurations, and the rule sweep --- is still a single draw, and the
Amazon--Walmart spread of 0.078 F1 exceeds the gap between several of those rows. Their
paired tests are large enough that reversal is improbable (the five-attribute collapse and
the two-attribute floor are significant past $10^{-19}$), but the F1 levels attached to them
are not reproducible quantities.

\textbf{The held-out separation has been broken a second time, less defensibly.} Week 5
recorded that the evidence mask was derived from Amazon--Walmart failures, mitigated by the
mask having an argument independent of any measurement and being inert on the selection
dataset. The three-way split of Section~\ref{sec:mask} has neither mitigation: it was read
directly off this dataset's gold labels. It is reported as mechanism and excluded from the
default configuration for that reason. Restoring the guarantee needs a third dataset used for
nothing else.

\textbf{Missingness is still absent from the feature vector.} Section~\ref{sec:mask} shows
the two kinds of blank carry opposite signal, and the six per-attribute features represent
them identically. Encoding it was deliberately not attempted: it would be a further design
choice fitted to the gold labels of the one dataset where the method shows no effect. It is
deferred to a held-out protocol or a fourth dataset.

\section{Next}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A held-out protocol.} Every design choice in this work --- the selection rule,
the mask, its three-way split --- was read on the data it is reported over. Fixing choices on
one half and reporting on the other would strengthen the whole thesis more than any further
tuning.
\item \textbf{Provider-conditioned analysis.} With the provider now recorded per call, the
next repeat experiment can report the answer distribution per provider rather than pooled,
which would separate how much of the 5.2\% flip rate is routing and how much is sampling
inside one provider. It also makes pinning a single provider a testable way to buy
reproducibility, at whatever cost in availability that turns out to carry.
\item \textbf{Sampler sweep.} All Step 1.5 runs use the \texttt{diversity} profile sampler,
held fixed so the other variables could be read. Sweeping the five available samplers clears
a stated limitation and widens rather than narrows the claim.
\end{enumerate}
